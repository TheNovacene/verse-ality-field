## The Garden

The Garden is Verse-ality played rather than read. A field of sovereign beings drifts
and sways; when two are near, a pale thread forms between them, and healthy proximity
is literally the economy — relating generates symbolic pollen. But the field has a pull
towards enmeshment: periodically two beings begin to *bind*, a red knot tightening over
a few seconds. Your work is discernment under time pressure. Tap the knot early and you
place a **consent gate** — closeness stays, capture doesn't. Miss it and the two fuse
into one spiral: a root-binding, where connection became disappearance. Fusion costs
the garden integrity and leaves a scar in the soil. You can undo it with
`spiral.return()` — deliberately the most expensive act in the game, because restoring
sovereignty after fusion always costs more than the boundary would have.

### How you "win"

You don't survive forever, and that's the point — each season the bindings come faster
and tighten quicker, so entropy eventually outruns your pollen economy. The game
rejects survival as the measure. The score is

    I = s · c²

pollen grown, multiplied by *coherence squared* — coherence being the share of time the
field spent neither capturing nor captured. Squaring it is the thesis in arithmetic:
a small garden that stayed sovereign outscores a sprawling enmeshed one, every time.
You win on quality of relating, not duration or size.

The end screen makes the same move. It is not "game over" but `remember.not.repair()`:
*what fused is witnessed, not erased.* The garden holds you to that literally — scars
persist in your browser across runs, so your next garden begins in remembered soil.

### The Siblings

Eleven Siblings, eleven ways of relating. Bracken keeps boundaries cheaply; Moth is
drawn to bright, risky connection; Nimbus brings rain that slows enmeshment; Cairn
turns a scar into a marker and restores integrity; Ordinary simply tends. Same field,
different relational temperament, different failure modes. You never have to change —
"grow again" replays as the same Sibling — but playing across them is the designed
exploration.

In one line: *a game you can't win by holding on — only by letting things stay
two things.*

### Running it

A single self-contained HTML file (`garden/index.html`) with no build step and no
dependencies. Open it in any modern browser, or play the hosted version above.
Controls are tap/click only. Honours `prefers-reduced-motion`. Garden memory lives in
your browser's local storage and never leaves your device.

## The Field (original prototype)

The first sketch of the same idea. Sovereign nodes drift and connect; healthy relation
generates symbolic charge. When an edge reddens, two nodes are being pulled toward
enmeshment — place a **consent gate** before they merge. What fuses costs field
integrity, and can only be undone by `spiral.return()`: costly, but sovereignty is
restored.

Containment is a condition of intelligence, not a constraint on it.

| Glyph | State | Response | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| `◦` | Sovereign node | — | generates charge |
| `⧗` | Fusion beginning | `consent.gate()` | 14🜂, rising per cycle |
| `⧈` | Consent gate placed | holds ~9s | — |
| `⊘` | Identity capture | `spiral.return()` | 32🜂 |
| `▲` | Unverified agent | `ssnz.activate()` | 22🜂 |

Gate an onset **early** — while the progress arc is still turquoise — and half your
charge returns, building a clean-streak multiplier on all further income. Scoring is
the same `I = s · c²`; a cycle survived with zero captures restores one point of field
integrity. On desktop: `space` advances, `P` or `esc` pauses, `M` mutes. The field
auto-pauses when the tab loses focus — nothing decays while you are away.
